# KCL grammar

Use stable IDs in briefs and findings. **Core** applies to all work; **Mode: reduced** describes that selected aesthetic; **Optional** applies only when chosen. Core rules are required. Mode rules are defaults that the brief can override for a visual reason. Optional devices are never completion requirements. Legacy numeric diagnostics describe a limited-ink plate; they do not certify beauty or apply to hybrid material automatically.

## GOV · Governing

- **GOV-1 Precedence — Core.** User intent, established project/approved brief, then defaults. Preserve accepted style/assets across edits.
- **GOV-2 Diagnosis — Core.** Judge concept, hierarchy and material quality together; repair the weakest layer. Do not assume every failure is missing geometry.
- **GOV-3 Diagnostics — Core.** Numbers ask questions; intentional negative space and nuanced tone can be correct. Record an explanation rather than falsifying a measurement or decorating to pass it.
- **GOV-4 Sources — Core.** Use supplied, generated, licensed or public-domain materials with provenance. Verify rights per sourced item; do not copy reference compositions or redistribute references in this skill.
- **GOV-5 Scope — Core.** KCL directs the graphic field. Keep readable text and controls functional; use the project's UI system or KFL for task interfaces. Do not pause the entire artwork because it contains a button.
- **GOV-6 Proof and authorization — Core.** Prototype composition and uncertain materials before final production. Record a sketch/material proof and the request or user approval authorizing continuation. User-requested approval gates remain binding; autonomy already granted does not require reconfirmation. Revisit only the affected region when actual material changes the composition.
- **GOV-7 Routing — Core.** Select supplied/generated/sourced/procedural/hybrid per element according to fidelity, precision and editability. Generators are available for supporting ornaments, surfaces and illustrated apparatus, not only characters. No source quotas.

## IMG · Concept

- **IMG-1 Concept sentence — Core.** State the visual idea and what each major material contributes. Subject/apparatus tension is one option, not a sentence template.
- **IMG-2 Visible relationship — Core.** Express the intended relation through contour, occlusion, scale, crop, alignment, color or material. Neither a compulsory apparatus nor compulsory body crossings defines success.
- **IMG-3 Economy — Core.** When a canvas feels weak, compare removal, scale, placement and replacement. Add imagery only when the concept needs it; add lines only when they relate things.
- **IMG-4 Marks — Optional.** Use graphic punctuation deliberately. Keep navigational icons in the functional UI; do not confuse decorative stars with controls.
- **IMG-5 Copy — Core.** Use meaningful, brief-led words. Omit invented technical labels, decorative English subtitles, duplicate headings and status text that contributes nothing. Fictional cataloguing is allowed when intentionally part of the world.

## SKL · Composition

- **SKL-1 Layers — Core.** Separate ground, planes, images, structure, accents, type and finish. The renderer's default order is a convenience; explicit `layer` overrides allow material both behind and in front of a subject. Split an asset when it must cross multiple depths.
- **SKL-2 Rhythm — Optional.** Use scale, spacing, density and repetition/variation. Curved dot paths are one device, not an obligation; exact equal spacing is valid for measured scales.
- **SKL-3 Lines — Optional.** Give every visible line a compositional or explanatory role. Protect important contours, faces and readable type. A leader can end at an object; a construction line can extend to an edge. No universal angle/count.
- **SKL-4 Planes — Optional.** Use blocks, strips, masks or paper fragments for balance and separation. Do not add bars solely to close a band.
- **SKL-5 Container — Optional.** Use a circle, arch, crop, silhouette or no container. Avoid a large circle behind every character.
- **SKL-6 Edges — Core.** Make containment, crop and overflow deliberate. Avoid accidental tangencies; not every frame needs a breach.
- **SKL-7 Scale — Core.** Make hierarchy visible. A 1:12 contrast is a reduced-mode starting point, not a universal minimum or reason to add tiny marks.
- **SKL-8 Punctuation — Optional.** Select a shape appropriate to the project. A repeated star border can become wallpaper; remove it when it has no role. No prescribed star shape or count.
- **SKL-9 Balance — Core.** Declare intentional symmetry or asymmetry. Distinguish a meaningful near-symmetry from an alignment mistake.
- **SKL-10 Fragments — Optional.** Use own-material fragments, detail crops or related specimens when they add narrative/scale. A subject need not shed anything; no fixed number.
- **SKL-11 Integration — Core.** Demonstrate a purposeful relationship between images and structure. Clipping, negative shape and shared contours can suffice. Pixel overlap is only one diagnostic; do not score integration by crossed pixels.
- **SKL-12 Density — Core.** Plan subject, structure, ornament and surface density separately. Protect an intentional quiet region. Fine image detail may supply the dense region without additional vector marks.
- **SKL-13 Weighting — Core.** Identify dominant mass and counterweight, including negative space. Follow the gesture and image silhouette, not just rectangular asset bounds.
- **SKL-14 Weight ladder — Mode: reduced.** Use hairline/line/rule/band/bar to establish contrast where useful. The historical three-rung/solid-plane check is a diagnostic, not a requirement to add a heavy ring.
- **SKL-15 Scale ladder — Mode: reduced.** Subject 55–140% and container 55–75% of the short side are prototype ranges only. Adjust to the brief and crop.
- **SKL-16 Richness — Core.** Judge craft and organized detail at the intended size. There is no required paper percentage, echo, textured rectangle or number of systems. Fine engraving cannot be replaced by coarse halftone merely to increase ink coverage.
- **SKL-17 Systems — Core.** Let repeated marks have an intelligible rule and purposeful variation. Repetition can show time, rhythm or taxonomy; it is not mandatory. Do not duplicate figures to fill space.
- **SKL-18 Relations — Optional.** Share centres/axes where geometry promises a common instrument. Multiple centres/gestures are valid for editorial spreads and collages. Avoid accidental near-relations; do not force every line in an artwork onto one axis.

## FLW · Reading

- **FLW-1 Route — Core.** Record how attention moves among priorities, whether linear, branching, orbital or clustered. A guide curve documents intention; it is not visible decoration.
- **FLW-2 Focal contact — Core.** The route must reach the important content, not necessarily intersect every box or cross the face.
- **FLW-3 Stepping stones — Optional.** Use image rhythm, gaze, type or marks to bridge distant stations. Empty intervals can also be intentional.
- **FLW-4 Exit — Optional.** A route may leave the frame, return or terminate at a meaningful focal point.

## COL · Color

- **COL-1 Roles — Core.** Assign palette roles and value/saturation hierarchy. Preserve approved illustration colors. `paper`, `ink`, `signal`, `signal2` drive existing renderer primitives; custom colors and preserved raster palettes are valid in hybrid mode.
- **COL-2 Accent budget — Mode: reduced.** A small signal area, often below 10%, is a starting point. No requirement to use it in both a hard and soft shape.
- **COL-3 Modes — Core.** Choose the material mode separately from palette treatment. Mono/Duotone suit reduced plates; hybrid can use a limited or full illustration palette without a user exception.
- **COL-4 Hue — Core.** Choose hue, temperature and chroma for the concept and project. Do not default to cobalt, vermilion or high saturation automatically.
- **COL-5 Effects — Mode: reduced.** Prefer flat planes over UI-like glow, bevel and glass. Natural shading in a drawn or photographed material is not a forbidden UI effect. Hybrid follows its Style Bible.
- **COL-6 Carrier — Optional.** When an accent must read at thumbnail size, give it adequate mass. A subtle accent or monochrome design need not pass a signal-blob minimum.

## MAT · Materials

- **MAT-1 Preserve or reduce — Core.** Preserve drawing/tones/color when they carry craft. Select threshold, dither, halftone, silhouette or line reduction only for an intended effect. Never impose “no midtones” on hybrid work.
- **MAT-2 Versions — Optional.** Use multiple treatments when they contribute meaning. One complete illustration is valid; no forced offset ghost, echo or red silhouette.
- **MAT-3 Edges — Core.** Preserve natural antialiasing, fine hairs and translucent ink. Remove unwanted matte/fringe without erasing delicate lines. Hard-alpha is an explicit reduced-mode option.
- **MAT-4 Preparation — Core.** Keep originals; export display-sized derivatives and inspect them. Do expensive raster processing before runtime where appropriate. A global filter does not create object-specific engraving.
- **MAT-5 Registers — Optional.** Classical, natural, technical, human and illustrated subjects can be mixed deliberately. Match perspective/drawing or make the contrast intentional; no fixed subject count.
- **MAT-6 Compositing — Core.** Choose RGBA, paper-backed multiply, masked image or tinted ink per asset. Multiply is not background removal and does not work identically on dark grounds. Tint only when intended.
- **MAT-7 Character — Core.** Preserve identity, gesture and useful rendering. A face is not a “softness allowance.” Unify through palette/material/placement; do not force reduction, body crossings or duplicate levels.
- **MAT-8 Supporting craft — Core.** When ornate detail, irregular mark-making or material behavior carries the artwork, use a suitable existing asset or generate it. Do not approximate it with generic paths just because code is available. Detailed supplied SVG is welcome.
- **MAT-9 Precision — Core.** Keep exact type, coordinates, ticks, branding and data in validated vector/DOM layers. Generated instruments are illustrative unless their exactness is independently verified.

## LAY / TYP · Layout and type

- **LAY-1 Structure — Core.** Choose or combine an appropriate structure from archetypes.md; custom structures are valid.
- **LAY-2 Quiet zone — Core.** Protect readable content from detailed material. Quiet does not mean filling the area with pale noise.
- **LAY-3 Reflow — Core.** Decide what crops, moves, reduces or disappears at narrow widths. A fixed poster can scale; an article's body text must remain readable.
- **LAY-4 Edges — Core.** Align according to the editorial grid and intended shapes; middle-distance placement can be intentional.
- **TYP-1 Roles — Core.** Set display, label, caption, body and experimental type as needed. Renderer roles `plane`, `mark`, `voice` are implementation buckets, not the limit of typography.
- **TYP-2 Pairing — Optional.** Start with a coherent display/text pair; verify actual glyph coverage and CJK/Latin proportions.
- **TYP-3 Distortion — Optional.** Distort only expressive display type, knowingly; preserve readable text.
- **TYP-4 Truth — Core.** Real data/labels must be accurate. Declare fictional catalogue systems in the brief; do not add meaningless pseudo-technical copy.
- **TYP-5 Voice — Optional.** Keep wording economical; no fixed two-sentence limit for editorial work.
- **TYP-6 Fonts — Core.** Record usable font sources/licences; verify actual rendered glyphs.
- **TYP-7 Type budget — Core.** Remove redundant type. More decorative captions are not a fix for an empty composition. For Japanese/CJK, check wording, vertical punctuation, mixed Latin orientation and line breaks; do not use unreadable foreign text as texture.

## TEX / MOT / SER · Finish, time and series

- **TEX-1 Local surface — Core.** Attach hatching to form, ink wear to print, and fibres to paper. Use an optional restrained global substrate to unify; do not cover all objects with the same dot pattern.
- **TEX-2 Softness — Optional.** Use zero, one or several soft materials as the concept requires. Control hierarchy, not a count.
- **TEX-3 Registration — Optional.** Use offset printing deliberately; do not use a thick displaced silhouette as automatic integration.
- **TEX-4 Depth — Core.** Make overlap, depicted volume and surface consistent with the Style Bible. Keep decorative UI shadows out unless the project calls for them.
- **MOT-1 Intent — Core.** Static is complete. Add a loop, reveal, scroll sequence or interaction only with a stated purpose and trigger.
- **MOT-2 Composition — Core.** Inspect start, end and meaningful extremes; preserve focal hierarchy and text readability.
- **MOT-3 Tempo — Optional.** Start with slow motion for print-like work; avoid simultaneous competing tempos.
- **MOT-4 Budget — Optional.** Three moving systems is a useful performance/attention starting point, not a universal maximum.
- **MOT-5 Timing — Optional.** Desynchronize ambient loops; synchronize a deliberate narrative sequence.
- **MOT-6 Performance — Core.** Prefer transforms/opacity and cheap masks; benchmark more expensive effects. Do not animate large filters without checking actual cost.
- **MOT-7 Gesture — Core.** Follow the material and intended motion. Avoid generic floating/bouncing of every layer.
- **MOT-8 Reduced motion — Core.** Provide a complete stable reduced-motion state, including script/SMIL animation where used.
- **SER-1 Variation — Core.** Share style; vary meaningful compositional decisions. Compare the series together to catch repeated templates.

## VER · Verification

- **VER-1 Visual evidence — Core.** Inspect actual target-size and narrow screenshots, thumbnail and material detail. Name missing checks.
- **VER-2 Numbers — Core.** Treat hue, coverage and overlap as descriptive. Reduced-mode thresholds are not valid aesthetic tests for full-color/hybrid artwork.
- **VER-3 Honesty — Core.** Not tested is not passed; prototype is not final; a plan for generation is not generated material.
- **VER-4 Motion evidence — Core when animated.** Check meaningful states/extremes, reduced motion and controls. Two nearby frames alone do not prove the whole sequence.
- **VER-5 Proof fidelity — Core.** Compare built relationships with the chosen proof; document justified adaptations to real assets.
- **VER-6 Pixel measure — Optional diagnostic.** Run when helpful to compare masses or regression. Never add dots, echoes or grids to satisfy paper/density thresholds. Hybrid reports preserve measurements without reduced-style warnings.
- **VER-7 Iterate visibly — Core.** Review the real composite after significant material/composition changes; a wireframe cannot certify craft.
