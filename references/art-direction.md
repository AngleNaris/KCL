# Art direction and Style Bible

Use before composing new work; for an existing project, extract decisions from actual assets and render first. Do not infer a redesign from a request to fix a page.

## Read references by role

| Role | Extract | Do not inherit automatically |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | face, costume, symbols, proportions | pose, lighting, layout |
| Drawing/material | line rhythm, hatching, edge character, shading | subject identity or exact ornament |
| Composition | hierarchy, mass, intervals, crop, negative shape | literal coordinates or motif inventory |
| Failure example | where hierarchy, craft or cohesion breaks | its circle/grid/texture recipe |

Describe what makes a reference convincing. “An engraved housing surrounds a precise measurement” is actionable; “add occult elements” is not. Build an original interpretation. Record sources without redistributing reference artwork inside the skill.

## Compact Style Bible

Record decisions that constrain subsequent assets:

- Concept and emotional effect; why each major material belongs.
- Drawing: contour weight, hatching direction/spacing, broken versus clean lines.
- Rendering: flat ink, tonal engraving, pencil, cel shading, paint, photographic collage; intentional combinations and their bridge.
- Shape: angular/organic balance, symmetry, gesture and negative shapes.
- Color: dominant masses, warm/cool relation, saturation/value hierarchy, preserved character colors, accent purpose. An accent can be muted or absent.
- Surface: which material belongs to paper, ink, metal, cloth, skin; local wear and optional common paper layer.
- Perspective/light: flat elevation versus volumetric view; direction and contrast.
- Detail: focal detail, secondary detail, quiet zones; intended size of a fine mark at display scale.
- Type: actual languages/words, display/caption/body hierarchy, vertical/horizontal treatment.
- Integration: contact points, occlusion, masks, crop, shared contours, protected face/text regions.
- Avoid: specific failure tendencies for this project, not a generic list of fashionable styles.

No universal mandatory palette, single soft element or one-centre geometry. Precision belongs where the picture promises precision; irregularity belongs where drawing and material need it.

## Material proof before final layout

When fine craft is essential and not already available, produce one representative supporting asset early: an engraved rosette, botanical cluster or ink edge. Test line quality and compatibility, not a full-page generation. Place it beside the hero and live type at intended size. Check whether detail survives, black mass is too heavy, and printing languages agree.

Use the result to refine layout or asset brief. Do not lock every coordinate then force an unsuitable asset into its box. Reuse the working Style Bible and relevant reference in later generation. Related parts can be one coherent assembly; independently animated/rearranged parts need independent outputs.

## Layered density

Describe density separately for **subject, structure, ornament and surface**. A detailed engraving can supply richness while structural lines remain sparse. An empty corner can be the counterweight. Paper percentage reveals neither quality.

Inspect thumbnail for mass/hierarchy, target size for type/line/rhythm, and detail crop for edges/material defects. Enlarge, remove, move or replace a weak element before adding another. More marks, images or smaller dots are not inherently more refined.

## Corrective examples

| Observed output | Diagnosis to test | Better decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Portrait with uniform star border and generic radial fan | Detailed hero surrounded by low-information repetition | Use purposeful engraved ornament if the concept calls for craft, or simplify the frame. Keep an exact fan when it serves a diagram. |
| Coarse halftone blocks enlarged behind the same portrait | Texture substitutes for imagery and destroys fine drawing | Preserve tones; use complementary material or a meaningful detail crop. Set halftone at a deliberate final dot size. |
| Diagonals cross face, type and hands | Geometry declares integration but harms hierarchy | Anchor a few meaningful relations, place some behind the figure, protect the focal contour and reading zones. |

These are possible causes visible in output; they do not establish how an unseen page was implemented.
